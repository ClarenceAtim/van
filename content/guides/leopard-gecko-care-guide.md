Leopard geckos (*Eublepharis macularius*) are gentle, hardy lizards from the dry grasslands of Pakistan, Afghanistan and northwest India. They're active at dawn and dusk, rarely bite, and can live **15–20 years**, so a good setup on day one pays off for a long time.

## Enclosure

- **Size:** a 36×18×18 in (90×45×45 cm) enclosure is the minimum we recommend for one adult. Bigger is always better.
- **Housing:** keep leopard geckos alone. Two males will fight, and pairs need careful management.
- **Substrate:** a loose, diggable mix of topsoil, sand and clay, or a bioactive arid substrate. Avoid calcium sand, which can cause impaction.
- **Hides:** provide at least three: a warm hide, a cool hide and a **humid hide** filled with damp sphagnum moss to help with shedding.

## Heat and temperature

Leopard geckos digest food and stay healthy by moving between warm and cool areas.

| Zone | Temperature |
| --- | --- |
| Warm side surface | 88–92°F (31–33°C) |
| Cool side | 72–78°F (22–26°C) |
| Night | Can drop to 65°F (18°C) |

Use a **halogen basking bulb** on a **dimming thermostat** as the main heat source, and check surface temperatures with an infrared thermometer. Never use a heat source without a thermostat.

## Lighting

A low-output UVB tube (5–6%) across part of the enclosure, on a 12-hour timer, supports natural behavior and vitamin D3 production. Always leave shaded areas the gecko can retreat to.

## Diet

Leopard geckos are insectivores. Offer a variety of **gut-loaded** insects:

- Dubia roaches, crickets and black soldier fly larvae as staples
- Mealworms and superworms in moderation
- Juveniles: feed daily. Adults: every 2–3 days.

Dust insects with **calcium + D3** at most feedings and a **multivitamin** once a week.

## Health checklist

- **Tail:** a plump tail shows good body condition.
- **Sheds:** check toes and tail tip after every shed for stuck skin.
- **Weight:** weigh monthly with a gram scale and note any steady loss.

If you notice weight loss, swollen limbs, or a gecko that refuses food for more than two weeks, contact a reptile vet.
