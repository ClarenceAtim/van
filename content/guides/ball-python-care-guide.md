Ball pythons (*Python regius*) are calm, slow-moving snakes from West and Central Africa. They're one of the most popular pet snakes because they stay a manageable size (3–5 ft) and tolerate handling well. With good care they live **25–30 years or more**.

## Enclosure

- **Adults:** a 4×2×2 ft (120×60×60 cm) enclosure. Juveniles can start in a 3×2×2.
- **Material:** PVC enclosures hold heat and humidity far better than glass with a screen top.
- **Security:** ball pythons are shy. Provide **two identical, snug hides** (one warm, one cool) plus plenty of clutter like cork bark and foliage. A snake that feels exposed won't eat.

## Heat

| Zone | Temperature |
| --- | --- |
| Warm side | 88–92°F (31–33°C) |
| Cool side | 76–80°F (24–27°C) |
| Night | No lower than 72°F (22°C) |

Overhead heat from a **radiant heat panel** or **deep heat projector** works best. Run every heat source through a **proportional thermostat**.

## Humidity

Keep humidity at **55–70%**. Use a moisture-holding substrate such as cypress mulch or coco fiber, and lightly mist when needed. A humid hide makes shedding easier. Low humidity is the most common cause of stuck sheds.

## Feeding

- Feed **frozen/thawed** rodents roughly the width of the snake's thickest point.
- Hatchlings: every 5–7 days. Adults: every 10–14 days.
- Thaw prey in the fridge, then warm it in a sealed bag in warm water. **Never microwave.**
- Use feeding tongs and offer the prey in the evening.

### Fasting is normal

Healthy adult ball pythons often refuse food for weeks, or even months, especially in winter or breeding season. As long as the snake keeps its weight, there's no need to worry. Weigh monthly and contact a vet if it loses more than 10% of its body weight.

## Handling

Wait a week after bringing your snake home before handling. Support its body fully and keep sessions to 10–15 minutes at first. Avoid handling for 48 hours after a meal.
