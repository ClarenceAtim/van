A **bioactive enclosure** is a small ecosystem. Live plants, soil microbes and tiny invertebrates (the "cleanup crew") break down waste and shed skin, so you clean far less and your reptile gets a richer environment.

## The layers

From the bottom up, a tropical bioactive build looks like this:

1. **Drainage layer:** 1–2 in (3–5 cm) of clay balls or similar, to catch excess water.
2. **Barrier:** mesh fabric so soil doesn't wash into the drainage.
3. **Bioactive substrate:** 3–4 in (8–10 cm) of a soil blend with organic topsoil, coco fiber, sphagnum and charcoal.
4. **Leaf litter:** a generous layer of dried oak or magnolia leaves. This feeds the cleanup crew and gives them cover.

Arid builds skip the drainage layer and use a sand, soil and clay mix that holds burrows. Keep the lower layer slightly moist so the cleanup crew survives.

## The cleanup crew

- **Springtails:** tiny insects that eat mold and fungi.
- **Isopods:** small crustaceans that break down waste, shed skin and leftover food. Pick a species that suits your humidity level.

Add the cleanup crew **2–4 weeks before your reptile** so their population can establish.

## Plants

Choose sturdy, non-toxic plants that suit your enclosure's light and humidity:

- **Tropical:** pothos, snake plant, bromeliads, peperomia, ferns.
- **Arid:** haworthia, gasteria, hardy succulents (avoid spiny or toxic species).

Use a full-spectrum plant LED for 10–12 hours a day alongside your reptile's heat and UVB.

## Maintenance

- Spot-remove large waste and uneaten insects.
- Mist to keep the target humidity, and let the top layer dry slightly between mistings in tropical builds.
- Top up leaf litter every few months as it breaks down.
- Trim plants as they grow.

## Is bioactive right for you?

Bioactive suits most geckos, snakes and small lizards. It needs more setup time and costs more up front, but most keepers find the enclosure easier to look after, and more interesting to watch, once it's established.
