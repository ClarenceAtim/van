Where your reptile comes from shapes almost everything that follows: its health, how easily it settles in, whether it eats, and how long it's likely to live. A healthy, **captive-bred** animal from a reputable source is the best start you can give a new pet, and it saves a lot of worry and vet bills.

This checklist explains what "captive-bred" really means, what to ask before you buy, and how to recognize a healthy animal.

<Note>
Even the healthiest animal benefits from a check-up with a qualified reptile veterinarian within the first few weeks, especially if you have other reptiles at home.
</Note>

## Captive-bred, captive-hatched and wild-caught

| Label | What it means | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Captive-bred (CB)** | Bred from parents kept in captivity, and hatched or born in captivity | Usually healthiest, used to captivity, often already eating standard foods |
| **Captive-hatched / farmed (CH or CF)** | Eggs or gravid females collected from the wild, then hatched in captivity | Higher risk of parasites; may be less settled |
| **Wild-caught (WC)** | Collected from the wild as adults or juveniles | Higher risk of parasites, stress and feeding problems; collection can harm wild populations |

### Why captive-bred is better

- **Fewer parasites and diseases**, because the animal has never been exposed to wild pathogens.
- **Already eating** captive foods such as frozen/thawed rodents, feeder insects or a powdered diet.
- **Better adapted** to people, enclosures and handling.
- **Known history**: hatch date, genetics, feeding and shedding records.
- **Better for wild populations**, because no animals are taken from nature.

## Questions to ask any seller

A good breeder or shop will happily answer all of these. Vague answers are a warning sign.

1. **Is it captive-bred?** Ask where and by whom.
2. **When was it hatched or born?** Very young animals are more fragile. Many sellers wait until hatchlings have eaten several meals.
3. **What does it eat, and when did it last eat?** Ask for the feeding record. For snakes, confirm it eats **frozen/thawed** prey and what size.
4. **How many times has it eaten for you?** Several consecutive meals is a good sign.
5. **When did it last shed, and was the shed complete?**
6. **What's its current weight?** Weigh it again when it arrives, with your own [gram scale](/shop/care-accessories/digital-gram-scale).
7. **Has it had any health problems or treatments?**
8. **For morphs, what are its genetics?** Ask about known genetic issues linked to some morphs.
9. **What support do you offer** after the sale?

## Signs of a healthy animal

Whether you're looking in person or at photos and video, check for:

- **Clear, bright eyes** with no swelling or discharge.
- **Clean nostrils and mouth**, with no bubbles, mucus or open-mouth breathing.
- **Good body condition**: rounded, not bony. In geckos, a **thick tail**.
- **Intact skin**, with no stuck shed, wounds, blisters or mites.
- **Alert behavior**: a snake that flicks its tongue and grips your hand, or a lizard that holds its head up and tracks movement.
- **Straight limbs, spine and jaw.**
- **Clean vent**, with no smearing or swelling.

Our article on [signs of a healthy reptile](/blog/signs-of-a-healthy-reptile) goes into more detail.

### Morphs

Morphs are color and pattern variations produced by selective breeding, from [tangerine leopard geckos](/shop/live-reptiles/leopard-gecko-tangerine) to [banana ball pythons](/shop/live-reptiles/banana-ball-python). Most are as healthy and easy to keep as the normal form, but a few morphs in some species are associated with genetic problems, such as neurological issues. Ask the seller, and research a morph before you commit.

## Buying online and shipping

Reptiles are routinely and safely shipped by overnight courier when it's done properly. Look for a seller who:

- Ships **overnight** in insulated boxes with heat or cold packs as needed.
- Only ships in **suitable weather**, and early in the week, so animals don't sit in a depot over the weekend.
- Offers a **live arrival guarantee** with a clear process if something goes wrong.
- Requires someone **18 or older** to receive the animal.

Plan to be home on delivery day, and have the enclosure set up and running at the right temperatures at least a few days beforehand.

## Legal and ethical checks

- **Check local laws.** Some species, or reptiles in general, are restricted in certain states, cities, rental properties or countries.
- **Check your lease** if you rent.
- **Avoid impulse purchases.** Many reptiles live 15–30 years or more.
- **Never release a pet reptile** into the wild. It's often illegal and harms native wildlife.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does captive-bred mean for reptiles?

Captive-bred means the animal was bred from parents kept in captivity and hatched or born in captivity. It's different from captive-hatched or farmed animals, whose eggs or mothers were collected from the wild.

### Is it safe to buy a reptile online?

Yes, if you buy from a reputable seller who ships overnight in insulated packaging, only ships in suitable weather and offers a live arrival guarantee. Ask for feeding records and photos before you buy.

### What age should I buy a reptile?

Many keepers prefer animals that have eaten several meals and are past the most fragile hatchling stage. Ask the seller for the hatch date and feeding history. A young, established feeder is usually a safe choice.

### Should I quarantine a new reptile?

Yes, if you have other reptiles. Keep the new animal in a separate room, with separate equipment, for at least 60–90 days, and wash your hands between animals.
