The [Russian tortoise](/shop/live-reptiles/russian-tortoise) stays relatively small, has a bold, curious personality, and is hardier than many tropical species. It is also a long-term commitment: with proper care it can live for 40 years or more.

<Note>
Every animal is an individual. Use this guide as a starting point and consult a qualified reptile veterinarian about any health concern.
</Note>

## Meet the Russian tortoise

Russian tortoises come from the dry steppes of Central Asia, where they spend the day walking, grazing and digging. Adults reach about 6–10 inches (15–25 cm), with males typically smaller than females. They are active, strong climbers and determined escape artists.

A healthy Russian tortoise patrols its enclosure, investigates anything new, walks with its shell lifted off the ground and shows a strong appetite for leafy greens.

## Housing: room to roam

A tortoise that walks all day needs floor space, not height. For one adult, start with at least a 4 × 2 ft enclosure, such as our [48 × 24 in tortoise table](/shop/enclosures/tortoise-table-48x24), and go larger if you can. Opaque sides help the tortoise feel secure and stop it from persistently trying to walk through glass.

Use a deep layer of soil-based substrate that holds a burrow without staying wet. Add a cool-side hide, a flat stone under the basking lamp, and a shallow water dish the tortoise can climb into easily. Keep everything escape-proof: these tortoises climb corners and push at weak lids.

## Heat and UVB lighting

Russian tortoises are sun worshippers. Provide a basking spot of about 90–95°F (32–35°C) under a halogen basking bulb, such as our [100 W halogen basking bulb](/shop/heating-lighting/halogen-basking-bulb-100w), with a cooler side around 70–75°F (21–24°C) so the tortoise can choose its temperature. Night temperatures can fall into the high 60s to low 70s Fahrenheit. Measure the basking surface directly rather than relying on air temperature alone. Our [reptile heating guide](/blog/reptile-heating-guide) explains how to set up and check a temperature gradient.

UVB is not optional. A desert-strength T5 UVB tube, such as our [T5 HO 12% UVB tube](/shop/heating-lighting/t5-ho-uvb-12-34in), mounted at the distance recommended by the manufacturer, lets the tortoise make vitamin D3 and use the calcium in its food. Run heat and UVB for a consistent daytime cycle of roughly 12–14 hours. Our guide to [UVB lighting](/blog/uvb-lighting-explained) covers tube choice, placement and replacement.

## Diet: leafy, fibrous and low in protein

Wild Russian tortoises graze on weeds and leafy plants. Recreate that with a varied diet of dandelion, plantain, clover, chicory, endive, escarole and other safe leafy greens. Variety matters more than any single green, and weeds from unsprayed areas often beat supermarket salad alone.

Avoid fruit, sugary vegetables and animal protein. Iceberg lettuce is mostly water and makes a poor staple. A little [grassland tortoise diet pellets](/shop/food-supplements/tortoise-diet-pellets) can supplement fresh greens in winter, but leafy food should remain the foundation. Dust food lightly with calcium regularly and keep a cuttlebone available. Our [supplements guide](/blog/calcium-and-vitamin-supplements-for-reptiles) explains calcium use in detail.

Feed a shell-sized pile of greens daily for growing animals and watch adult body condition. Slow, steady growth with a smooth shell is the goal; rapid growth on a rich diet can contribute to shell pyramiding.

## Water, soaking and daily care

Keep clean drinking water available at all times. Many keepers also soak their tortoise in lukewarm, shallow water for 15–20 minutes a few times a week, more often for juveniles, always supervised. Soaking encourages drinking and normal toileting.

Spot-clean droppings and uneaten food daily, checking the tortoise as you do: clear eyes and nostrils, a firm shell and a good appetite are reassuring signs. Weigh it regularly and record the numbers, because gradual weight loss is easy to miss.

## Winter and brumation

Wild Russian tortoises brumate through cold winters, but a healthy captive tortoise kept warm indoors does not have to. Never brumate an underweight, young or unwell animal, and get veterinary advice before attempting it. Our guide to [brumation](/blog/brumation-explained) explains how to prepare safely if you and your vet decide it is appropriate.

Get the basics right — space to walk, a hot basking spot, strong UVB, a leafy high-fibre diet and clean water — and a Russian tortoise is a rewarding, characterful pet that can share your home for decades.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big do Russian tortoises get?

Adults usually reach about 6–10 inches (15–25 cm) in shell length. Males are typically smaller than females, and most growth happens in the first several years.

### What should a Russian tortoise eat?

A varied diet of leafy greens and safe weeds, such as dandelion, plantain, clover, chicory and endive. Avoid fruit, sugary foods and animal protein, and supplement calcium regularly.

### Do Russian tortoises need UVB light?

Yes. They need desert-strength UVB to make vitamin D3 and absorb calcium properly. A T5 UVB tube mounted at the manufacturer's recommended distance, replaced on schedule, is the standard choice indoors.

### How often should I soak my Russian tortoise?

A supervised soak in lukewarm, shallow water for 15–20 minutes a few times a week suits most animals, with more frequent soaks for juveniles. Always keep drinking water available in the enclosure as well.
