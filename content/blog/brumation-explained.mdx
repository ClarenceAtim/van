Every autumn, some keepers panic: their bearded dragon stops eating, hides all day and barely moves. In most cases the animal is not sick. It is brumating — the reptile version of hibernation, a natural winter slowdown triggered by shorter days and cooler temperatures.

<Note>
This article is general information, not a diagnosis. Illness can look a lot like brumation, so if you are unsure, contact a qualified reptile veterinarian before assuming your animal is brumating.
</Note>

## Which reptiles brumate?

Not every pet reptile brumates. Species from temperate climates do — most famously **bearded dragons**, many **tortoises** (such as Hermann's and Russian tortoises) and some temperate snakes like corn snakes. Tropical species such as ball pythons, crested geckos and leopard geckos generally do not brumate, and a tropical animal that goes off food in winter is more likely to be too cold or unwell. Check the care guide for your species before deciding what is normal: our [bearded dragon care sheet](/blog/bearded-dragon-care-sheet) covers brumation for dragons specifically.

Brumation usually starts in late autumn. The animal eats less, basks less, spends more time in its hide and may sleep for days at a time. Young animals under a year old often skip brumation or only slow down briefly, and many keepers keep juveniles warm and feeding normally through their first winter.

## Before brumation: the health check

Never let a sick or underweight reptile brumate. A healthy animal goes into winter with fat reserves to live on; a sick one can get much worse while its immune system is slowed down. In the weeks before your reptile slows down:

- **Get a vet check.** A reptile vet can confirm your animal is a healthy weight and free of parasites. This is the single most important step.
- **Weigh your animal.** Use a [digital gram scale](/shop/care-accessories/digital-gram-scale) and write the weight down. You will compare against this number all winter.
- **Stop feeding before the sleep.** Once your reptile stops eating on its own, or about one to two weeks before you expect it to settle, stop offering food while keeping the basking spot warm. Food left undigested in a cold gut can rot, so the digestive tract needs to be empty before temperatures drop.
- **Keep fresh water available.** A brumating reptile still needs water, and some will wake briefly to drink.

## During brumation: cooler, darker, quieter

Brumation is driven by temperature and light, so the enclosure needs to change gradually, over one to two weeks, not overnight. Shorten the light cycle by an hour or two and let temperatures fall to the cool range for the species — around 60–70°F (15–21°C) for a bearded dragon, cooler still for many tortoises. A [digital thermometer and hygrometer](/shop/care-accessories/digital-thermometer-hygrometer) with a probe lets you check the cool end without opening the enclosure and disturbing the animal, and a thermostat like our [proportional thermostat](/shop/heating-lighting/proportional-thermostat) keeps the new lower temperatures steady instead of swinging with the room.

Check on your animal every week or two, briefly and gently. Weigh it every couple of weeks: a small loss is normal, but losing more than about 10% of body weight is a reason to warm the animal up and call a vet. Offer water during checks, and make sure the water dish stays clean and full. Otherwise, leave the animal alone — waking it repeatedly burns the energy it is trying to save.

## Waking up in spring

As days lengthen, most reptiles wake on their own over a week or two. Warm the enclosure back up gradually and return the light cycle to normal. Offer water first, then small meals once the animal is basking and moving normally again. Appetite usually returns within a few days. If your reptile stays lethargic, refuses food for more than a week after warming up, or has discharge around the nose or mouth, see a vet — those are signs of a respiratory infection, not a slow wake-up.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does brumation last?

It varies by species and by individual. Bearded dragons commonly brumate for a few weeks to three or four months, while some tortoises sleep for three to five months. Young animals often brumate for a shorter time or not at all.

### Should I wake my reptile up during brumation?

Only briefly, for a weekly health check and a weigh-in. Keep handling to a minimum and put the animal straight back. Repeated waking burns fat reserves and stresses the animal.

### Do I need to feed my reptile while it brumates?

No. A brumating reptile should not be fed, and its gut should be empty before it settles. Always keep fresh water available, and offer food again only once it has woken up and warmed up in spring.

### Is my reptile brumating or sick?

Brumation happens in autumn or winter in a healthy animal that has been checked by a vet. Weight loss over about 10%, breathing problems, mucus, swollen eyes or lethargy in a warm enclosure all point to illness. When in doubt, see a reptile vet rather than assuming brumation.
